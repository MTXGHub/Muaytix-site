[guide.h2]
Which Rajadamnern seats should you choose?

[guide.h3.ringside]
Choose Ringside if you want to feel close to everything

[guide.p1.ringside]
Ringside is the closest public seating to the ring. You will hear the corner instructions, see the fighters and officials working at close range, and feel the movement of the fight night around you.

[guide.p2.ringside]
Front row is the closest and most immersive position when available. Sections 3 and 4 are closest to the fighter entrance walkway.

[guide.h3.club-class]
Choose Club Class if you want the easiest choice

[guide.p1.club-class]
Club Class combines an elevated view with a numbered seat. It is simple to understand, comfortable for families and practical for groups who want to sit together.

[guide.p2.club-class]
MuayTix generally recommends Row D and above for the clearest elevated perspective, although the lower rows still provide a clear view of the ring.

[guide.h3.leo-section]
Choose LEO if atmosphere matters most

[guide.p1.leo-section]
LEO faces the front of the ring and gives you a strong view of the entire canvas.

[guide.p2.leo-section]
On Wednesday, Thursday and Sunday, the section also carries the sound and movement of the local betting crowd. You do not need to take part in betting. You can simply watch the action and experience the atmosphere around it.

[guide.h3.third-class]
Choose Third Class if you want the whole arena

[guide.p1.third-class]
Third Class is at the highest point of the stadium. Instead of focusing only on the ring, you see the event as one large picture: the fighters, walkout, corners, crowd and arena around them.

[guide.p2.third-class]
It opens according to demand, so availability changes from one event to another.

[faq.h2]
Rajadamnern Stadium seating questions

[faq.q1]
Are there bad views at Rajadamnern Stadium?

[faq.a1]
No. The stadium was designed as an amphitheatre around the ring, so the seating provides clear sightlines. Each section offers a different perspective rather than a different standard of view.

[faq.q2]
Do unassigned seats mean I might have to stand?

[faq.a2]
No. Every ticket guarantees a seat. Unassigned seating means you choose your position within the section when you arrive.

[faq.q3]
Which seats are best for families?

[faq.a3]
Club Class is usually the easiest choice for families because it has numbered seats, an elevated view and convenient access to food, drinks and toilets.

[faq.q4]
Is Ringside really close to the ring?

[faq.a4]
Yes. Ringside seats are on the floor and only a few metres from the ring. Officials, cameras and production staff may work nearby as part of the live fight-night environment.

[faq.q5]
Is LEO Section the betting section?

[faq.a5]
LEO is where the local betting crowd gathers on Wednesday New Power, Thursday Petchyindee and Sunday Kiatpetch. This atmosphere does not apply to every night of the week, and guests do not have to take part in betting.

[faq.q6]
Are LEO and Third Class standing areas?

[faq.a6]
No. Both sections provide a seat for every ticket. Guests choose their position within the ticketed section.

[faq.q7]
Which rows are best?

[faq.a7]
MuayTix generally recommends Row D and above for Club Class, LEO and Third Class. At Ringside, there is no universal best row. Front row is the closest and most immersive position when available.

[faq.q8]
Can children attend?

[faq.a8]
Children can attend all four public seat classes. Club Class is usually the most straightforward choice for younger children. LEO and Third Class can suit families who prefer a livelier atmosphere or a wider view of the stadium.

[faq.q9]
Is there a dress code?

[faq.a9]
There is no formal smart-casual dress requirement. Dress sensibly for a sporting event inside an air-conditioned arena. The stadium can feel cool, so a light extra layer may be useful.
