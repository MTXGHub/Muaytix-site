The ﬁve problems the rebuild solves
1. Visibility without selection. The parent seating terms sit at positions 7 to 10. At that
depth, a title that says "seating" is not enough. The page needs to be the answer to
"which seats should I choose", not a description of seating.
2. Two intents on one page. Map queries and choice queries currently point at one
page. Splitting them lets each page say exactly one thing in its title and ﬁrst
paragraph, which is what moves position 7 to position 3.
3. No product pages. Ringside has 131 impressions and 0 clicks because there is no
Ringside page. Searchers with money in hand ﬁnd nothing speciﬁc.
4. Section-number blindness. Searchers say "section 10", "section 11", "section 8 or 9".
If those relationships are not stated in plain sentences, neither Google nor an AI
answer engine can conﬁrm them.
5. Booking is not seat-led. Guests worry about the seat ﬁrst, then the event. The
current ﬂow makes them choose in the wrong order.
Two corrections to the brief, from the source of truth
The brief lists "section 11 view" queries under Third Class and "seat view" under the
parent. Fine, but the Source of Truth also gives MuayTix a distinctive, defensible
point of view on rows (Row D and above in Club Class, LEO and Third Class; front

row ﬁrst at Ringside). That row guidance is the single most differentiating piece of
content in this whole system and does not appear in the keyword brief at all. It has
been built into every child page.
"Best seats" queries (rajadamnern stadium best seats, 5.15 per cent CTR at position
5.96) are the highest-converting wording in the parent cluster. The parent page
answers "best seats" by priority, not by crowning one class, exactly as the brief
requires.

PART 4: Conversion architecture and booking-widget
strategy
Principles applied
Resolve uncertainty ﬁrst, then offer the action. On every page the widget arrives
after the guest knows what it is, where it is, what they will see and who it suits.
One widget per page, never repeated. Repetition on a phone reads as pressure. A
sticky "Book Tickets" button on mobile scrolls to the single widget instead.
Seat-led on the hub, date-led on the children. On the hub the guest picks class then
date. On a child page the class is already chosen, so the only decision is the date.
Trust near the top. Guests ask "where will I sit" and "who am I buying from" early. A
one-line trust strip (international ticket partner for Rajadamnern Stadium, seat class
and row guidance included) sits under the hero on the hub and product pages.

Which Rajadamnern seats are best? It depends what you
want
There is no single best seat at Rajadamnern. There is a best seat for the night you want.
Best for being as close as possible
Ringside. Sections 3 to 7 sit on the ﬂoor of the main arena, a few metres from the
ropes. If you want to feel the impact of every strike, this is the section. Sections 3 and 4
sit either side of the ﬁghter entrance walkway, so they are the closest seats to the
walkouts. [Link: Ringside seats at Rajadamnern]
Best for a numbered seat and an easy night

Club Class. Sections 8 and 9 are elevated, every seat is numbered, and facilities are a
short walk away. It is the most popular choice with MuayTix guests and the most
straightforward option for families. [Link: Club Class, Sections 8 and 9]
Best for atmosphere
LEO Section. Section 10 faces the front of the ring and is where the local ﬁght fans
gather on the three traditional Muay Thai nights: Wednesday, Thursday and Sunday. It is
also where the broadcast cameras sit, so the view straight down the ring is the one you
see on television. [Link: LEO Section, Section 10]
Best for seeing the whole stadium
Third Class. Section 11 is the top of the building and shows you the entire arena at
once. It opens when demand ﬁlls the main arena, which is exactly when the atmosphere
is at its strongest. [Link: Third Class, Section 11]

Rows: how MuayTix places you
The section decides the experience. The row decides how well you see it. MuayTix
places guests with that in mind.
Ringside: allocated from the front row backwards. Front row is the closest and
most immersive position and most guests ask for it, so we allocate it ﬁrst unless

you tell us otherwise.
Club Class: we recommend Row D and above. It lifts you clearly above the walkway
eye line for an elevated view of the whole ring.
LEO Section: seating is unassigned, so you choose. We suggest around Row D and
above for the best overall sightline. Arriving earlier simply gives you more choice of
position, including the front row when it is free.
Third Class: Rows A to C face a safety fence. Row D and above gives you the
clearest view over the arena.
Assigned and unassigned seating explained
Ringside and Club Class are assigned. Your seat number is on your ticket.
LEO Section and Third Class are unassigned. That means you choose where to sit
within the section when you arrive. It does not mean seating is limited and it does not
mean anyone stands. There are no standing areas at Rajadamnern Stadium, and every
ticket guarantees a seat. [Link: how unassigned seating works in the LEO Section]

Good to know before you book
The stadium is fully air-conditioned and can feel cool. A light extra layer is worth
bringing.
There is no formal dress code. Dress sensibly for a sporting event indoors.
Personal photography and video are allowed. Flash between bouts only, not during
a ﬁght.
Food, soft drinks and beer are sold inside, and can be served directly to Ringside
seats.
Children are welcome in every seat class. Club Class is usually the easiest choice
with younger children.
Rajadamnern is compact. Photographs make the distances between sections and
the ring look bigger than they feel in person.
Rajadamnern Stadium seating: your questions

What are the seat classes at Rajadamnern Stadium?
Four public classes: Ringside (Sections 3 to 7), Club Class (Sections 8 and 9), LEO
Section (Section 10) and Third Class (Section 11). VIP areas exist but are not covered
on this page.
Which seats are best at Rajadamnern Stadium?
It depends on the night you want. Ringside for proximity, Club Class for a numbered
seat and an easy night, LEO Section for atmosphere and the broadcast view, Third Class
for the whole-stadium picture. There are no bad views anywhere in the building.
Is Club Class or LEO Section better?
They share the same tier and a similar elevation. Club Class gives you a numbered
plastic seat and is the calmer choice for families. LEO gives you concrete tier seating,
the choice of where to sit, a front-facing view and, on Wednesday, Thursday and Sunday,
the local betting crowd and the atmosphere that comes with it.
Do I have to arrive early for LEO Section or Third Class?
No. Every ticket guarantees a seat. Arriving earlier only increases your choice of
position within the section.
Can children sit in every section?
Yes. There is no age restriction in any of the four classes. Club Class is normally the
easiest with younger children because of the numbered seats and nearby facilities.
How many people does Rajadamnern Stadium hold?
About 3,078 spectators: roughly 192 at Ringside, about 1,000 across the main arena
including Ringside, Club Class, LEO Section and VIP, and about 2,100 in the upper tier.
Can I choose my exact seat when I book?
Ringside and Club Class are assigned seats. Tell MuayTix your preference and we will

Rajadamnern Stadium Seat Map
Rajadamnern Stadium is a compact amphitheatre built around the ring. Ringside sits on
the ﬂoor, Club Class and the LEO Section sit on the elevated tier of the main arena, and
Third Class sits in the upper tier at the top of the building. The map below shows where
each section is.
[FULL-WIDTH SEAT MAP: all sections numbered, four classes in their class colours, ring
in the centre, ﬁghter entrance walkway marked]
Caption: Rajadamnern Stadium seating plan. Ringside Sections 3 to 7 (green), Club
Class Sections 8 and 9 (blue), LEO Section 10 (gold), Third Class Section 11 (orange).
How the Rajadamnern Stadium layout works
The stadium was designed so that every seat looks down or across at the ring with
nothing in the way. There are no internal pillars anywhere in the building.
There are three seating levels. The ﬂoor of the main arena is Ringside. Above it, the
elevated tier of the main arena holds Club Class and the LEO Section on the same row
conﬁguration. Above that, the upper tier holds Third Class, the highest seats in the
stadium.
The whole building is smaller than it looks in photographs. Distances between the tiers
and the ring feel shorter in person.
Rajadamnern Stadium sections explained
Where is Ringside? Sections 3 to 7
Ringside is on the ﬂoor of the main arena and wraps around most of the ring. The raised
ﬁghter entrance walkway runs between Sections 3 and 4, which makes those two
sections smaller than Sections 5, 6 and 7 and puts them closest to the walkouts.
Ringside seats are assigned.

Where are Sections 8 and 9? Club Class
Club Class covers Sections 8 and 9 on the elevated tier of the main arena. Every seat is
numbered. The traditional Muay Thai orchestra plays from the rear of Section 9.

/rajadamnern-stadium-seats/club-class
Where is Section 10? LEO Section

The LEO Section is Section 10. It sits on the same elevated tier as Club Class and faces
the front of the ring directly. The principal broadcast cameras and the commentary
team are positioned here. Seating is unassigned, with a seat guaranteed for every
ticket.
[Zone card: LEO highlighted] [Link] What it is like to sit in the LEO Section →
/rajadamnern-stadium-seats/leo-section
Where is Section 11? Third Class
Third Class is Section 11, the upper tier at the highest point of the stadium. There is no
seating above it. It holds about 2,100 guests and opens on busier nights as the main
arena ﬁlls. Seating is unassigned, with a seat guaranteed for every ticket.

Why the stadium is shaped like this
Rajadamnern was commissioned in 1941. Construction paused during the Second
World War, and the ﬁrst ﬁght took place on 23 December 1945. It began life as an open-
air amphitheatre in the Roman style and was later given a concrete roof, but the seating
bowl is essentially the one designed eighty years ago: every spectator with a clear line
to the ring.
The stadium marked its 80th anniversary in December 2025. Since 2022 it has been
operated by Global Sport Ventures, who launched the Rajadamnern World Series as part
of its modernisation.
Seat map questions
Which sections are elevated at Rajadamnern Stadium?
Club Class (Sections 8 and 9) and the LEO Section (Section 10) sit on the elevated tier
of the main arena. Third Class (Section 11) is higher still, in the upper tier.
Is the LEO Section the same as Section 10?
Yes. LEO Section and Section 10 are the same area. It was sometimes called Second
Class in older descriptions.
Is Third Class the same as Section 11?
Yes. Third Class, sometimes written 3rd Class, is Section 11.
Are Sections 8 and 9 both Club Class?
Yes. Club Class is made up of Sections 8 and 9.
Is there standing room at Rajadamnern Stadium?
No. There are no standing areas anywhere in the stadium. Every ticket, including
unassigned LEO Section and Third Class tickets, guarantees a seat.
Which seats are closest to the ﬁghter entrance?
Ringside Sections 3 and 4, on either side of the raised walkway.

The view from Ringside
You are at ring level. Fighters are at eye height, close enough to see every detail of

What sets Ringside apart is what you hear. Strikes land with a sound you do not get
further back. Corner instructions between rounds are audible. The cornermen, judges
and production crew are working within a few metres of you.
If you are in the front row, you may ﬁnd a judge, a member of the production team or a

Muay Thai event is the whole point.
Food, soft drinks and beer are available throughout the night and can be served directly

Ringside questions
Is the front row the best Ringside seat?
It is the closest and most immersive position, and the one most guests request. Other
rows remain excellent. MuayTix allocates front row ﬁrst when it is available unless you
ask for something else.
Will ﬁghters walk past me at Ringside?
The entrance walkway runs between Sections 3 and 4, so those sections are closest to
the formal walkout. What happens after a bout varies by ﬁght and corner, so interaction
cannot be guaranteed.
Are Ringside seats assigned?
Yes. Your seat is assigned when you book, and MuayTix places you from the front row
backwards unless you tell us otherwise.
Can children sit Ringside?
Yes. There is no age restriction. Ringside is a busy, close and loud environment, so Club

LEO Section at Rajadamnern Stadium (Section 10)
The LEO Section is Section 10 at Rajadamnern Stadium. It sits on the elevated tier of
the main arena, directly facing the front of the ring, and seating is unassigned: you
choose where to sit when you arrive, and every ticket guarantees a seat. This is the

Where Section 10 is
Section 10 is on the same elevated tier as Club Class, with effectively the same
elevation and row layout, and it faces the front of the ring straight on. It was sometimes
called Second Class in older descriptions of the stadium; today the section is the LEO
Section.
The principal broadcast cameras and the commentary team are positioned in the LEO
Section. That is the simplest way to understand the view: it is the angle you see when
you watch Rajadamnern on screen.
[Zone map: Section 10 highlighted] [Link: See the LEO Section on the Rajadamnern

The view from the LEO Section

the whole ring, and see both corners and the walkouts. MuayTix generally recommends
around Row D and above for the best overall sightline. Rows below are good seats too;
Row D and above simply clears the walkway in front of the section completely.
[Photo: view from LEO Section, Row D or above]
[Photo: view from the LEO Section front rows]
Choosing your own seat
LEO seating is unassigned. That means the choice of position is yours, not that seats
are limited. There are no standing areas at Rajadamnern and every ticket in Section 10
comes with a seat.
Arriving earlier is never required. It only gives you more choice, including the front row
of the section when it is free. If you want a speciﬁc row or a spot near the cameras,
come a little earlier. If you just want to watch the ﬁghts, arrive when you like.
The seating itself is concrete tiered seating rather than a plastic seat base. It is the

Third Class at Rajadamnern Stadium: Section 11
Third Class is Section 11 at Rajadamnern Stadium, the upper tier at the very top of the
building. From here you see the whole arena in one picture: the ring, the walkout, the
corners, the main arena and the crowd. Seating is unassigned with a seat guaranteed
for every ticket. Third Class opens according to demand, so it is available on some
nights and not others.
[Button] Book Third Class Tickets →  #book
Trust strip
Booked through MuayTix, the international ticket partner for Rajadamnern Stadium. If
Third Class is on sale for your date, you will see it in the booking widget below.
Where Section 11 is
Section 11 is the upper tier of Rajadamnern Stadium. It sits above the main arena,
above Club Class and the LEO Section, and there is no seating level higher. The upper
tier holds about 2,100 guests, more than the whole of the main arena combined.
Facilities are a longer walk than from the main arena sections. The reward is the
highest, widest view in the stadium.

[Zone map: Section 11 highlighted] [Link: See Section 11 on the Rajadamnern Stadium
seat map]
The view from Section 11
From Third Class the stadium becomes one scene. You watch the ﬁght and, around it,
everything that makes a Rajadamnern night: the walkouts, the corners at work, the
crowd in the main arena below, the orchestra, the whole amphitheatre. It is the view for
guests who want to see the event rather than only the ring.
There are no pillars and nothing structural in the way. The sightline from the
recommended rows is clear across the whole arena.
[Photo: view from Third Class, Row D or above]
[Photo: view from Third Class showing the full arena]
Rows and the safety fence
Rows A to C at the front of Section 11 face a safety fence. It is a normal feature of an
upper tier that holds 2,100 people, there to protect guests in the tier and those seated
below.
MuayTix recommends Row D and above. From there you are beyond the fence rows and
get the clearest view over the arena. Because seating is unassigned, you choose your
row when you arrive.
[Photo: Third Class rows showing the safety fence and rows behind]
When Third Class opens
Third Class does not open every night. It opens according to demand and is highly
seasonal.
Busiest nights: Saturday nights are the most consistent example of Third Class
opening at some point as the stadium ﬁlls.
Low season: Third Class may open only for the biggest nights.
High season: as overall attendance rises, Third Class can open much more often,
potentially across most nights of the week.
Availability is never permanent, so MuayTix does not promise it in advance. If Third
Class is on sale for your date, it will show in the booking widget. If it is not, the LEO
Section and Club Class are the closest alternatives, and both are covered further down
this page.

What Third Class feels like
Third Class opens when the main arena is ﬁlling, which means it is at its best on the
nights the whole stadium is alive. The upper tier becomes its own crowd, looking down
on a full house. For a repeat visitor, or anyone who wants the feeling of being inside
Rajadamnern on a major night, it is a different and complete view of the event.
[Photo: Third Class on a full night]
Choosing your own seat
Seating in Section 11 is unassigned. You choose your position within the section when
you arrive. This does not mean seating is limited and it does not mean standing. There
are no standing areas at Rajadamnern, and every Third Class ticket guarantees a seat.
Arriving earlier only gives you more choice of row.
Who Third Class suits
Guests who want to be inside Rajadamnern on a big night and see the whole event
from above
Visitors who prefer a wide view of the whole arena to maximum proximity
Repeat visitors who know the stadium and want a new perspective
Photographers looking for a high, wide angle
Guests comfortable choosing their own row within the section
Children are allowed in Third Class. Club Class or the LEO Section will usually be more
engaging for younger children because they are closer to the ring, but Third Class
remains a valid option when it is what is available.

If Third Class is not on sale for your night
The LEO Section, Section 10, sits on the elevated tier of the main arena, faces the front
of the ring and also lets you choose your own seat. It is the closest experience to Third
Class in terms of freedom, with a nearer view. [Link: The LEO Section at Rajadamnern
Stadium]
Club Class, Sections 8 and 9, is on the same elevated tier with a numbered seat and
easy access to facilities. [Link: Club Class at Rajadamnern Stadium]
[Link: Compare all four Rajadamnern Stadium seat classes]
Section 11 questions
Is Third Class the same as Section 11?
Yes. Third Class, sometimes written 3rd Class, is Section 11, the upper tier of
Rajadamnern Stadium.
Is Section 11 open every night?
No. It opens according to demand. Saturday nights are the most consistent, high
season brings it open more often, and in low season it may open only for the biggest
events. The booking widget shows the dates it is on sale.
Is there standing in Third Class?
No. Every Third Class ticket guarantees a seat. Seating is unassigned, which means you
choose your position.
What is the safety fence in Section 11?
A fence in front of Rows A to C that protects guests in the upper tier and those seated
below. MuayTix recommends Row D and above for the clearest view.
Can children sit in Third Class?
Yes. Club Class or the LEO Section are usually more engaging for younger children
because they are closer to the action.
How far is Section 11 from the ring?
It is the highest tier in the stadium. Rajadamnern is compact and the view over the
arena is clear, but this is the section for seeing the whole event rather than for being
close to the ring. For proximity, look at Ringside. [Link: Ringside seats at Rajadamnern
Stadium]
