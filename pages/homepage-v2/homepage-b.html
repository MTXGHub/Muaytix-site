Saturday nights

Rajadamnern World Series

RWS is Saturday night at the stadium: seven professional bouts, Thai and international fighters, and the judges' scores shown after every round so anyone can follow it. It is the busiest night of the week and the one most visitors come for.

The rest of the week

Six more nights, five promoters

A different promoter takes the ring each night, each with its own format and crowd.

  • Sunday Kiatpetch Muay Thai. Traditional five-round Muay Thai from one of Thailand's established promoters.
  • Monday, Tuesday and Friday Rajadamnern Knockout. Seven three-round bouts, an earlier finish and the easiest first night.
  • Some Mondays All Star Fight.
  • Wednesday New Power Muay Thai. Nine five-round bouts and the loudest crowd of the week.
  • Thursday Petchyindee Muay Thai. Hand-picked fighters and a slower, technical pace.

First time?

You do not need to know the rules. Every bout has a clear winner, the crowd tells you when something big happens, and the night runs two to three hours. Dress is casual, children are welcome, and you can arrive after the first bell.

Where to sit

Four seat classes, one ring

Ringside, Club Class, LEO Section and Third Class all watch the same fights. The difference is how close you are and the crowd around you.

The venue

The world's first Muay Thai stadium

Rajadamnern Stadium opened in 1945 and still hosts professional Muay Thai every night of the week. Modern lights, screens and a dome show now sit inside the original ring and tiered seating.
